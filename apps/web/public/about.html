About BLOKDUO

A free block puzzle you can play on your own, or two-up on one board with somebody else, live.

What it is

BLOKDUO is an 8×8 block puzzle. You are given three pieces at a time and you drop them onto a grid. Fill a whole row or column and it clears. Run out of room for every piece you are holding and the game is over. It takes about ten seconds to learn.

It runs in a browser. There is no download, no account, no email address to hand over, and nothing to install before you can play. Open the page and the board is there. Your progress is saved against an identifier generated on your device, which is why you are never asked who you are.

The part that is different

Most block puzzles are solitaire. BLOKDUO has a second mode, Duo, where two people play the same board at the same time — not two boards side by side, and not turns taken hours apart. You share one grid and one tray of three pieces, and you take alternating turns with a clock running.

Sharing a tray is what makes it a different game rather than the same game with a scoreboard. The piece you leave behind is the piece your partner has to use. A gap you can see a use for is a gap they might fill by accident. Playing well together means thinking about what you are handing over, which is not a thing solitaire block puzzles ever ask you to do.

Starting a game is a link. One player makes a room, sends the link, and the other is in the game — no accounts, no friend requests, no lobby to find each other in.

Why it exists

Block puzzles are everywhere and most of them are the same game with different colours, wrapped in adverts that interrupt the thing you opened the app to do. BLOKDUO was built to answer a narrower question: what does this genre look like if two people can play it together in real time, and if the interruptions are optional?

The rules are deliberately strict about that second part. Nothing forces you to watch an advert to keep playing, and declining one never costs you progress you already earned. The game is free, and the parts of it that cost anything cost time you chose to spend.

How honest scoring works

Scores are not reported by the browser. When a game finishes, the whole game — every piece, every placement, in order — is sent to the server, which replays it under the same rules and works out the score itself. A client that makes up a score has to make up an entire playable game to go with it.

That is why the leaderboard is worth looking at, and why the rules on the how to play page are the real ones rather than a description of them.

Who made it

BLOKDUO is an independent project, built and run by one person in Canada. It is not affiliated with any other block puzzle game or its publisher. There is no company behind it, no investors, and no roadmap written by anyone else.

If something is broken, or you have an idea, the contact page goes to a real person.
